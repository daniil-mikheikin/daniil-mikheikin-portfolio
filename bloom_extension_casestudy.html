Case study — Bloom Extension

A focus kit that fits in a 400px popup

Bloom is a productivity browser extension that ends app switching by bringing a Pomodoro timer, to-dos, notes and ambient soundscapes into the browser you are already working in — in three modes, for three ways of working.

01Backstory

Bloom’s backstory

Bloom is a productivity browser extension built to solve the problem of switching between applications and to take the inefficiencies out of a browser workflow.

It did not start there. It grew out of two earlier projects of mine, each of which solved half of the problem.

Install Bloom from the Chrome Web Store ↗Designed and built from scratchCode written with Claude Code
02Context

Context

Modern professionals spend a large part of the workday inside a browser — researching, studying, managing their tasks.

Yet the browser’s UI and UX fall short of what those people need. They face constant distraction and app switching — even though…

… the browser has effectively become an extension of the physical workspace, and should therefore feel comfortable and productive.

03Challenge

Challenge

Build an all-in-one productivity tool as a browser extension that fits a compact 400px popup, and works as a seamless extension of the browser workspace.

It also had to be highly adaptable, because a tool that suits only one way of working gets uninstalled by everyone else.

Diverse work stylesDiverse needsNo page real estate to spareMust feel native to the browser
04Solution

Solution

I built an all-in-one productivity tool as a browser extension that brings together a Pomodoro timer, to-dos, notes and ambient soundscapes — a complete focus kit housed in a compact 400px popup.

To support different kinds of work, it carries three distinct modes rather than one fixed layout.

The prototype is a 1200px browser window holding a 400px popup, so it needs a desktop screen. Open this page on one to try it.

05How might we

Asking the right questions

The starting point in finding the right solution was asking the right questions about the problem.

  • 01How can we design a browser interface that minimises mental overload and promotes sustained, focused work?
  • 02How can we integrate focus, task management and a calm environment into the browser so that users stay in flow without leaving their main workspace?
  • 03How can we turn the browser from a source of distraction into a single environment for productive work?
Problem statement

People stop using concentration apps not because they don’t work — they stop because these apps don’t fit into the digital workspace. They feel cold, inflexible and forgettable.

06Two axes

Modes and themes are not the same thing

This is the single easiest thing to confuse when you look at the screenshots. Modes change what the popup does. Themes change how it looks. They are independent, so a screenshot can show Classic mode in the Pumpkin theme — and most of the ones below do.

Modes — what it does
  • Classic modeA calm, minimal workspace — timer, notes, to-dos, music and tab groups. The everyday default.
  • Research modeDesigned for deep reading — typography settings, focus mode, and a reading progress bar.
  • Creative modeInspect any page — click elements to grab their colors and fonts, then save them for your own projects.

07Classic mode

Classic mode

Classic mode is for ordinary days. The hard part was fitting every tool an everyday user needs into a popup no taller than a postcard, without any of it feeling crowded.

  • Pomodoro timer
  • To-do list
  • Notes
  • Ambient sounds
  • Tab groups
01

Pomodoro timer

Open the menu and choose one of the preset rhythms — Classic 25 / 5, Deep work 50 / 10, Flow 90 / 20 — or set your own, then pick how many focus and break cycles to run.

It leaves the popup

Once a session starts, the timer detaches onto whatever page you are on and stays there while you work — draggable to any corner, so it never sits on the sentence you are reading.

02

To-do list

Type a task and press Enter, or hit the plus. Tick items off as you finish them; a counter keeps the score and one action clears everything already done.

03

Notes

A plain scratchpad that autosaves as you type, with a live word count. Deleting is the one destructive action in Bloom, so it is the one action that asks first.

04

Ambient sound

When background sound helps, Bloom carries nine loops of its own — and will take a link to whatever else you listen to.

RainForestWhite noisePianoFireplaceStormSnowstormBirdsOcean

Nine built-in loops, plus a custom source — paste a YouTube, Spotify or audio link. Sound plays through an offscreen document, so it keeps going after the popup closes.

05

Tab groups

The Tools menu can gather your open tabs into a named group. It is the one feature that reaches out of the popup and changes the browser itself — the group appears in the real tab bar.

08Research mode

Research mode

Research mode is built for long articles and documents. It stops being a panel of its own tools and becomes a set of controls over the page you are already reading — the type, the width, the colour of the paper, and how much of it is lit at any moment.

  • Focus readingDim the page, spotlight the text
  • Reading screen modeCompress the page into a reading column
  • TypefaceSeven faces, including OpenDyslexic
  • Type controlsWeight, size, line height, letter, word and column width
  • Reading time + progressA bar across the top of the page
  • Page tintDefault, Creamy, Sepia, Blue, Green, Rose, Midnight
09Creative mode

Creative mode

Creative mode turns the whole web into a swatch library. Point at any element on a page and Bloom reads back its colour and its type; click, and both are saved to the toolbar for your own work.

Step 1Inspect

Turn on Inspect, then hover the page. Every element reports its colour and font as you pass over it.

Step 2Grab

Click to capture. The exact hex and the real font name, size and weight are read off the element itself.

Step 3Keep

Captures stack up in the toolbar, each one copyable, ready to paste into your own project.

10Themes

Six ways for it to feel like yours

One of the goals was to make the browser’s workspace feel comfortable for everyone, and comfort is not something you can settle centrally. Beyond the tools, Bloom needed to be able to look like the person using it.

Each theme is a full token set — background, two surfaces, text, muted and faint text, two borders, an accent and its soft and glow variants — so switching one restyles every screen at once rather than recolouring a header.

  • Classic#5b6b8cQuiet, neutral, out of the way
  • Sakura#e07ea0Soft petals, pastel calm
  • Pumpkin#d2823fWarm autumn, paper & spice
  • Ocean#3fa1c9Bright shore, easy blue
  • Midnight#6d83d6Classic dark, easy at night
  • Gothic#c52338Dark-fantasy, blood & gold
Gothic goes further

Gothic is the only theme with decoration beyond its tokens: a heavy outer shadow, an inset gold hairline, gold corner brackets drawn from eight gradients, a crimson crest and a wordmark with a gold gradient clipped to the letters. It ships as its own build.

11Reflection

Final thoughts

This project was a real adventure for me, since I built it all by myself from scratch. Projects like this are always the most valuable, because they are the very source of growth — in the uncertainty and the lack of specific knowledge that you have to acquire in order to accomplish the task at hand.

As part of the Bloom extension project, I needed to gain a deeper understanding of how browsers work and their limitations, and learn how to apply that knowledge to improve my product. It was an amazing project, and I don’t regret doing it one bit.

  • Built the whole thing aloneFrom research and UX through UI, brand and shipping.
  • Learned the platform, not just the screensHow browsers actually work, and where their limits are.
  • Grew in the uncertaintyThe knowledge I lacked at the start was the point of doing it.
